# What Does a GT Anywhere Academic Advisor Actually Do?

In a program where a platform handles the teaching, the Academic Advisor is the person who knows your child. That makes this role the one worth understanding most carefully, because it is where the model either holds up or does not.

## The job in one line

The platform decides what your child learns. The Advisor decides whether it is working.

## What they actually track

An Advisor is looking at a continuous stream of data that a classroom teacher never gets: exactly what your child worked on, for how long, what they mastered, what they retried, and what they quietly avoided.

That last one matters more than it sounds. A child who has not touched writing in nine days is invisible in a traditional classroom, because they were present and compliant every one of those days. In GT's data, it is obvious.

So the Advisor watches for:

- Pace falling off, which usually means something got hard or something outside school changed
- Pace that is suspiciously fast, which often means a placement is too easy and the child is coasting
- A subject being avoided
- Mastery that is not sticking on retest
- Daily targets being missed in a pattern rather than a one-off

## What they do about it

Four things, roughly.

**Meet.** A standing one-to-one with your child every two weeks. Not a status report. A conversation about how it is going, what is hard, and what they want to be aiming at.

**Intervene.** When the data says something is wrong, they reach out rather than waiting for the check-in. A misplacement gets adjusted. A stuck student gets a human.

**Remove obstacles.** A lot of the job is unglamorous and practical: a placement that needs redoing, a workshop that conflicts with something, a technical problem that has quietly blocked a week of work.

**Plan the arc.** GT frames this as trajectory work, showing a family how this year connects to where the child is going in three years. For a fifth grader that might mean naming the competition or the piece of work they are building toward by eighth grade.

## What they are not

They are not delivering lessons. If you are picturing a virtual teacher walking your child through fractions, that is not this role, and it is not how the model works. See [what the platform does and what the humans do](does-ai-teach-my-child-at-gt-anywhere).

They are also not a substitute for a parent keeping the daily rhythm intact, particularly for younger students. The Advisor sees the data and meets the child. They are not in your house at 9am.

## The question to ask on your call

The honest weak point of any advisor model is caseload. An advisor watching a manageable number of students catches the eleven-day writing avoidance. One watching too many catches it in month two.

GT's ratios are structurally wider than a campus program, and the reasoning is that homeschool families already supply daily structure and motivation, so the Advisor's job is tracking and trajectory rather than daily instruction. That reasoning is sound as far as it goes. It also means the model leans on your household more than a campus would.

Ask directly: how many students does my child's Advisor carry, and what specifically triggers them to reach out unprompted?

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a GT Anywhere Academic Advisor do?

They track your child's daily progress data, meet with them one-to-one every two weeks, remove obstacles, and plan the longer arc of where the child is heading. They coach rather than deliver lessons, since the platform handles content.

### How often does my child meet their Advisor?

GT reports biweekly one-to-one check-ins, with asynchronous contact in between. The Advisor is also watching the daily data continuously, so contact is not limited to the scheduled meeting.

### How does my child get help when they are stuck?

The platform adapts first, re-approaching the material or stepping back to the prerequisite the student actually missed. When a student is stuck in a way the platform cannot resolve, the pattern surfaces in the Advisor's data and a human reaches out.

### Will siblings share the same Advisor?

Advisor assignment depends on grade band and cohort placement, so siblings may or may not share one. Ask admissions how it would work for your specific children before enrolling.
