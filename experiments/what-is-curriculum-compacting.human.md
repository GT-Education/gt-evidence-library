

# What Is Curriculum Compacting?

**Curriculum compacting is a teaching strategy that removes material a student has already mastered and replaces it with more challenging or enriching work.** Instead of making a highly achieving or gifted child sit through lessons on skills they have already developed, the teacher "compacts" the known content and frees that time for something new. It was developed specifically for high-achieving students, and the research concludes that teachers can eliminate **40–50% of the regular curriculum** for these students **with no loss in achievement**.

## How it works

Compacting has three key components:

1. **Identify** what a student already knows in a subject with a pre-test.
2. **Eliminate** the instruction for skills they've already mastered.
3. **Replace** that time with more advanced material, deeper projects, or other enrichment.

The goal isn't to give a child *less*, but rather to give them something *more* worth their time instead.

## Why it matters for gifted kids

For many high-achieving students, a large proportion of the school year is review. In the **1993 Curriculum Compacting Study** comprised of **436 teachers across 27 districts**, the National Research Center on the Gifted and Talented found that when teachers removed up to **50%** of the regular curriculum for high-ability students, there was **no significant difference** in achievement compared with their peers who learned from an unchanged curriculum. In fact, the **compacted math and science groups scored significantly higher.** Moreover, **78–88%** of above-average 5th–6th grade readers passed pretests on skills *before those skills were even taught* ([NRC/GT, 1993](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED379847.pdf)).

The takeaway: without compacting, a capable child can spend most of their year waiting. This leads to the boredom and disengagement many parents of gifted kids notice. 

## Example

Imagine a 4th grader who already knows multi-digit multiplication as confirmed by a pre-test. Instead of three weeks of redundant practice worksheets, the teacher compacts that unit, allowing the student to spend that time on an early introduction to fractions or a deeper mathematics project. Although it is the same class and same schedule, the ceiling for learning is now raised.

## Related terms

- **Academic acceleration**: moving a student to higher-level content or grade placement.
- **Mastery-based learning**: advancing only when material is mastered rather than a fixed time interval.
- **Under-challenge**: the problem compacting is designed to solve.



## Frequently asked questions



### What is curriculum compacting?

Curriculum compacting is a teaching strategy that removes material a student has already mastered and replaces it with more challenging or enriching work. It was developed for high-achieving students who have already mastered most of the standard grade-level content.

### Does curriculum compacting hurt achievement?

No. In the landmark NRC/GT study, teachers eliminated around 40–50% of the regular curriculum for high-ability students with no significant difference in achievement. In fact, compacted math and science groups scored significantly higher.

### Who is curriculum compacting for?

Curriculum compacting is built for students who have already mastered a meaningful portion of the grade-level curriculum. Oftentimes, these are gifted or high-ability learners who would otherwise spend class time reviewing what they already know.

### How is compacting different from acceleration?

Compacting removes already-mastered content within a course so a student can go deeper into a subject or learn at a faster pace. Acceleration moves a student to higher-level material or a higher grade placement. The two are related and often used in conjunction.
