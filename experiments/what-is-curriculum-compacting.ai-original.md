# What Is Curriculum Compacting?

**Curriculum compacting is a teaching strategy that removes the material a student has already mastered and replaces it with more challenging or enriching work.** Instead of making a high-ability child sit through lessons on skills they already have, the teacher "compacts" the known content and frees that time for something new. It was developed specifically for high-ability students — and the research behind it is striking: teachers were able to eliminate **40–50% of the regular curriculum** for these students **with no loss in achievement**.

## How it works

In practice, compacting has three steps:
1. **Identify** what a student already knows in a subject (often with a pretest).
2. **Eliminate** the instruction and practice for skills they've already mastered.
3. **Replace** that time with more advanced material, deeper projects, or enrichment.

The goal isn't to give a child *less* — it's to stop making them relearn what they know and give them something worth their time instead.

## Why it matters for gifted kids

For many high-ability students, a large share of the school year is review. The **1993 Curriculum Compacting Study** — a national study of about **436 teachers across 27 districts** run by the National Research Center on the Gifted and Talented — found that when teachers removed up to **50%** of the regular curriculum for targeted high-ability students, there was **no significant difference** in achievement compared with peers who did all the work. In fact, the **compacted math and science groups scored significantly higher.** In the same study, **78–88%** of above-average 5th–6th grade readers passed pretests on skills *before those skills were taught* ([NRC/GT, 1993](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED379847.pdf)).

The takeaway: without compacting, a capable child can spend much of the year waiting. That's a direct cause of the boredom and disengagement many parents of gifted kids notice.

## Example

Imagine a 4th grader who already knows multi-digit multiplication. A pretest confirms it. Instead of three weeks of practice worksheets, the teacher compacts that unit and the student spends the time on an early introduction to fractions, a data project, or a math competition problem set. Same class, same schedule — but the ceiling moves up.

## Related terms
- **Academic acceleration** — moving a student to higher-level content or grade placement (compacting is often paired with it).
- **Mastery-based learning** — advancing only once material is mastered, rather than by seat-time.
- **Under-challenge** — the problem compacting is designed to solve.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is curriculum compacting?
It's a teaching strategy that removes material a student has already mastered and replaces it with more challenging or enriching work. It was developed for high-ability students who already know much of the grade-level content.

### Does curriculum compacting hurt achievement?
No. In the landmark NRC/GT study, teachers eliminated 40–50% of the regular curriculum for high-ability students with no significant difference in achievement — and compacted math and science groups scored significantly higher.

### Who is curriculum compacting for?
Students who have already mastered a meaningful portion of the grade-level curriculum — often gifted or high-ability learners who would otherwise spend class time reviewing what they already know.

### How is compacting different from acceleration?
Compacting removes already-mastered content within a course so a student can go deeper or faster; acceleration moves a student to higher-level material or grade placement. They're related and often used together.
