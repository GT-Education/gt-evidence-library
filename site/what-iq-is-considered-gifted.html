What IQ Score Is Considered Gifted?

Published July 24, 2026 · Updated July 24, 2026 · 4 min read

The honest answer is that there is no single official IQ cutoff for "gifted." Giftedness is not defined by one number, and because there is no federal definition, the bar shifts by state and by program. That said, some ranges come up again and again: many gifted programs use the top 10% (roughly an IQ of 120 and up), or the 95th to 98th percentile (around 125 to 130), as a working guide. "Highly gifted" is often cited near 145, and "profoundly gifted" around the 99.9th percentile. Useful signposts, not a verdict.

What the numbers actually mean

IQ scores are built so that 100 is average, and most people fall in the middle. The further out you go, the rarer the score:

  • Around 120 and up sits near the top 10%, the guide NAGC mentions for identification and services1.
  • Around 130 sits near the 98th percentile, the bar many gifted programs use.
  • Around the 99.9th percentile is "profoundly gifted," the top 1 in 1,000 on IQ and achievement tests2.

Percentile is often the clearer way to think about it: the 98th percentile means your child scored higher than 98 out of 100 same-age peers.

Why the number is only part of the picture

A score is a snapshot, not the whole child. NAGC points out that IQ tests capture the intellectual domain but are not as good at spotting creative or leadership ability, and that scores should be read against current, non-biased, often local norms3. Two children with the same overall score can have very different needs. So treat the number as one input among many, alongside the everyday signs you already see at home.

One caution for twice-exceptional kids

If your child is twice-exceptional, gifted and also has something like ADHD or dyslexia, a single full-scale IQ number can actually hide their giftedness, because a learning challenge can drag the overall average down. NAGC specifically warns that 2e students can be overlooked when only the general score is used, and recommends looking at subscores3. If the number and your gut disagree, that gap is worth exploring, not ignoring.
