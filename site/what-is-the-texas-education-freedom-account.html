What Is the Texas Education Freedom Account (ESA)?

Published July 24, 2026 · Updated July 24, 2026 · 4 min read

The Texas Education Freedom Account (TEFA) is the state's new education savings account program. For eligible Texas families, it puts state funds into an account you can use for approved education expenses, including tuition at participating private and online schools. The 2026-27 award is $10,474 per student. Whether your family qualifies depends on the program's rules, so always confirm current eligibility with the official state source before you count on it.

Key takeaways

  • TEFA is Texas's state-funded education savings account (ESA).
  • Eligible families receive funds for approved expenses, including private and accredited online school tuition.
  • The 2026-27 award is $10,474 per student.
  • Eligibility and funding are not guaranteed; confirm with the official state program.

What the Texas Education Freedom Account is

An education savings account works differently from a traditional school budget. Instead of funding only a public school seat, the state deposits money into an account that families can spend on approved education costs. Texas is part of a fast-growing national trend: by the 2025-26 school year, more than 1.5 million students were participating in private school-choice programs across 34 states, plus D.C. and Puerto Rico.

How much it is worth

For the 2026-27 school year, the TEFA award is $10,474 per student. Award amounts are set by the state and can shift year to year, so treat that number as specific to that school year rather than a permanent figure. Always check the current amount before you plan around it.

Who qualifies, and what to check

This is the part to get right. Eligibility is defined by the state program and can depend on things like residency and enrollment status, and in a new program, demand can exceed the funds available. Please do not assume you qualify or that funding is automatic. Confirm current eligibility and application details on the official Texas Education Freedom Account site, and make sure you have an actual award in hand before you commit to a school.

What you can use it for

ESAs are meant to cover approved education expenses. In practice that usually includes tuition at participating schools, including accredited online schools, and often other qualified costs like curriculum or certain services. The official program spells out exactly what counts, so use that list as your source of truth.

How this works for an accredited online school

Here is where it gets practical for many families. Because an accredited K-8 online school can be priced at or under the award, the ESA can cover most or all of tuition. GT reports that its online option, GT Anywhere, is tuition of $10,400 per year, just under the $10,474 award, which GT describes as effectively $0 net for eligible Texas families.
